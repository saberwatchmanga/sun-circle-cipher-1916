# Evidence chronology and corrections

## Prior work

S. Tomokiyo, “Solution of Two Chinese Cipher Telegrams Archived in Japan (1916)” (first posted 18 September 2026; last modified 23 September as observed on 7 October), identifies the exact target and worksheets, publishes selected readings, and derives the three-kana construction using this target and the Huang Xing telegram. The key and those partial readings predate this project. Bourdeau's repository is a valuable record of AI-assisted attempts and source leads; its “unsolved” labels are not an exhaustive scholarly adjudication.

## 7–8 October: source collation and independent opening print

The raw Roman copy, analyst kana and pencil glosses were separated. Original-image cross-page joins and several previously omitted formula words were checked. The 1916 welcome-telegram print was located through secondary leads and read on its original page. The fixed first107 comparison preserves absent 我, header differences, incorrect/uncertain Roman letters, bracket notation and e ambiguity. The eight signature spans were located before this print was retrieved; five match raw Roman letters and six match worksheet numbers. The shuffled control includes a permutation tying the positive count, so it does not prove uniqueness.

Finite phrase searches, correction lattices, language-model beam searches, vowel-contraction proposals and context edits produced candidates but did not establish a general historical error channel. A global t→k change damaged 34 of the 107 calibration characters. Scanned OCR could not reliably recover even known Roman groups and was rejected as letter-repair evidence. None of those paths supplies a completed later plaintext. This release reruns only the bounded checks documented here; it does not claim to reproduce all historical search experiments.

## 9 October: third-round audit

An AI review claimed strong support for 韶入贑 and 入湘贑, described a chosen-text scan as blind, inferred a six-person list, and suggested roughly nine-tenths completion. The audit identified these problems; the fourth-round response explicitly retracted or downgraded them:

- 韶 7300 (ヱコナ) and 蘿 5700 (エコナ) both Romanize to `ekona`. The scan cannot choose between them. Its first-kana boundary convention is also explicit.
- The stripped-source windows [896,911) and [896,912) end inside the final word. Each gives distance 1; consuming complete [896,913) `kaesomikanuteashi` against `kaesomikanuteasu` gives distance **2**. The ≤1 full-word claim is withdrawn.
- The witness texts were selected after their pages and candidate readings had been seen. The program was fixed but the texts were not blind. Shuffled controls do not control this selection step.
- 軍務院 was already read and is a method check. A three-character match is not three independent decipherments. Four-character scan matches were zero in that bounded test.
- Mixed literal/stripped/consensus offsets cannot be used as unified raw coordinates.
- Completion percentages and “the gist is fully understood” are withdrawn. No defensible denominator or independent complete plaintext existed.
- Signature slot 5 is unresolved, not an established 楊永泰. The independent published reply's recipient footnote instead suggests 趙正平 and conflicts with other identifications; recipients of a reply are not automatically signers of the target.

The independent publication of Huang Xing's 25 May reply was checked in *近代史資料*, total issue 50, printed p.123 / PDF p.125, item 3 (“致肇庆军务院电”). Editorial dates in parentheses and recipient footnotes are later editorial evidence, not original manuscript labels. This is an already known reply, not the target's complete 20 May text. *軍務院考實* vol.1, printed p.70 / PDF p.175, is another telegram with related military content; it is thematic evidence, not a verbatim target witness. 6381 is blank in the checked 1915/1926 codebooks: that limits a glyph lookup, but does not identify its intended correction.

## 9 October: frozen fourth round

Predictions were hashed before new archive images were retrieved. All five third-volume catalogue units overlapping 15 May–15 June were screened (259 PDF frames). No qualifying independent same-key text or second target copy was confirmed. P1/P2/P3 remain untested. Research on the exposed later text was paused; publication does not resume speculative decoding.
